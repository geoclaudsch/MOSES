Rivers represent a significant source of the greenhouse gases (GHGs) carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), and nitrous oxide (N₂O). The ratios between the 3 gases, 
which determine the climate impact of a river, is highly variable both in time and space. We monitored the three GHGs in the German river Elbe over 5 years to identify 
spatio-temporal pattern of GHG ratios as well as to uncover the mechanisms shaping the relative contribution of the three gases to river GHG emissions.

The total GHG-potential of the three gases in terms of CO2 equivalents was dominated by CO2 and its seasonal cycle. Higher CH4 emissions during summer were compensated by 
CO2 uptake. We successfully used our dataset to develop data-driven models based on machine-learning methods, reaching coefficients of determination greater than 0.75. 
In river Elbe it is probably possible to predict GHG concentrations based on seasonal indicators without the need for water quality parameters.

The folder **seasonal_dynamics_control_GHG_in_Elbe_river** contains the script we developed for the ML analysis of our data.
